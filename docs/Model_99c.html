The token grant is a membership credential with no cash value inside the system. All wallet custody, token placement and revocation terms are disclosed in the ULA.
01

The loop that closes on itself

A user pays 99¢. Apple takes its cut. From what is left we pay Solana account rent, a transfer fee, and grant the user some quantity of $ACM as their membership credential. Everything remaining is profit, and 100% of profit buys more $ACM — which raises the price, which makes the next user's grant more expensive. The system funds its own cost inflation.

Rent is modelled as non-recoverable. It is refundable only by closing the account, which means shutting the system down — and a user who changes phones leaves a wallet we cannot reclaim. For a going concern it is a cash outlay, and every download is a new wallet even when it is the same human on a second device.

04

The answer

10,000,000 downloads
Every one of them profitable, at $0.4325 per download below the $1M Apple threshold and $0.2840 above it. Total banked over the pool's life: $2.84M – $4.33M. The wall arrives not from weak demand but from the pool running dry.

Your wallet holds 11,767,037 ACM. Releasing 10,000,000 as the funding pool and holding 1,767,037 as R&D reserve gives ten million downloads of free inventory at one token per user — every grant costs nothing, and the margin is the full headroom.

05

What stops it — and it is us

Because 100% of profit buys $ACM, by the time the pool empties at ten million downloads that buying has lifted the price from $0.000265 to $18.62 — a factor of 70,266. The next token then costs $18.62 against $0.2840 of headroom.

Buying $ACM with the profits is what prices the program out of its own token. The app makes the asset valuable and is then unable to afford the thing it made valuable.

The R&D reserve is the other side of that coin: 1,767,037 ACM at $18.62 is $32.9M on paper. That is a real number and an unsellable one — a $22.7K pool cannot be exited at anything near that price, because the same (1 + C/R)² curve runs in reverse on the way out.

06

The three levers

And one that is not a lever: rent consumes 41% of gross at every scale. It is fixed in dollars, it is the largest cost after Apple, and it does not come back. Not minting a wallet until a user actually engages would recover more than any other change on this page.

Ten million paid downloads is an extraordinary outcome for a nostalgia app. The model says you would reach it profitably — and that the thing which finally stops you is a policy choice you control, not the market.

Read the other direction, this is a constructive result. Instagram drew 762 million downloads globally in 2025; ten million is a little over one percent of that, and every one of those ten million pays for itself. The binding constraint is not demand and not Apple — it is how much $ACM leaves the pool per user, and what the profit does next. Both are yours to set.
